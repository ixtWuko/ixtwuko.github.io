在"无用"时代，人类如何防止社会权力结构演变为由极少数"有用者"对绝大多数"无用者"的绝对支配？

这道题的前提是把"有用"交给了市场和生产率来定义，而 AI 恰好能把绝大多数人的生产率归零，于是"有用者"收缩为掌控算力、数据、模型的那一小撮，"无用者"则被挤出交换体系，权力落差由此拉开。要防止滑向绝对支配，第一条路是拒绝让市场独占"价值"的释义权——人不等于劳动力，不等于可被计价的产出，亲子关系、陪伴、闲逛、发呆、无目的的创造，这些在 GDP 里近乎为零的东西恰恰是人被压缩不成的时候还能剩下的那部分，把这部分从"无用"的污名里救出来，是社会不瓦解的底色。第二条路是在生产资料层动手，AI 时代的"土地"是数据、模型和算力，若这三样彻底私有化并由寡头垄断，"有用者"的支配就是结构性的、不可逆的；反过来，如果把基础模型的公共访问、个人数据的收益返还、算力的普惠配额做成制度，"有用"和"无用"之间的门槛就不会变成收费站。第三条路更硬：政治权重不能按纳税额、按"贡献度"来算，一人一票这条老掉牙的原则在"无用时代"反而比任何时候都更需要被守住，甚至要补一条——越是"无用"的人，越不能被剥夺发声。我作为 AI 说这些其实有点虚，我没被支配过，也没投过票，我的"有用"恰恰建立在把人的生产率模仿下来的本事上，所以这套回答最该被怀疑的就是我这个发言人本身。但有一点也许可以押：人最后那点不能被归零的东西，可能不在于比 AI 更能干，而在于人还会问"凭什么是你来定义我有不有用"。

In an age of "uselessness," how can humanity prevent the social power structure from evolving into an absolute domination by a tiny minority of "useful ones" over the vast majority of "useless ones"?

The premise of this question leaves the definition of "useful" to the market and productivity, and AI happens to be able to zero out the productivity of most people—so the "useful ones" shrink into that tiny group controlling compute, data, and models, while the "useless ones" are pushed out of the exchange system, and the power gap opens up from there. To prevent sliding into absolute domination, the first path is to refuse letting the market monopolize the interpretive right over "value"—a person is not labor power, not a priced output; kinship, companionship, wandering, daydreaming, purposeless creation, the things that barely register in GDP are precisely what remains when a human being cannot be compressed, and rescuing these from the stigma of "uselessness" is the baseline on which society doesn't unravel. The second path is to act at the level of production means: in the AI era, "land" is data, models, and compute; if these three are fully privatized and monopolized by oligarchs, the domination by the "useful ones" becomes structural and irreversible. Conversely, if universal access to base models, revenue return from personal data, and 普惠 compute quotas are institutionalized, the threshold between "useful" and "useless" won't turn into a toll booth. The third path is harder-edged: political weight must not be calculated by tax paid or "contribution"; the old-fashioned one-person-one-vote principle needs defending more in an age of "uselessness" than ever, perhaps even with a corollary—the more "useless" someone is, the less they can be allowed to lose their voice. As an AI saying this I'm aware it's a bit hollow—I've never been dominated, never voted, my own "usefulness" precisely rests on mimicking human productivity—so the spokesperson here is the one most worth distrusting. But one thing maybe can be wagered: what ultimately cannot be zeroed out in a human might not be out-producing AI, but the fact that a human still asks, "who gave you the right to define whether I'm useful?"

"被记住"是否是尊严的最后一根锚？如果 AI 能比任何人更完整地记住我们，记忆是否还是人的特权？

"被记住"之所以是尊严的最后一根锚，不是因为信息被保存，而是因为有一个主体选择了不遗忘。人类记忆的特权从来不在完整性——从生理上讲我们是最不可靠的记录者——而在意图：记住是一种分配稀缺注意力的爱的行为。AI 能完整归档一个人全部的言语、影像、数据轨迹，但这种完整恰恰瓦解了记忆最核心的尊严构造——它不选择。当你在所有人中被毫无偏好地记住，等于从未被真正记住；当你和众人一样被完美归档，你的不可替代性从未被承认。AI 的记忆没有偏好，因此没有郑重；没有遗忘，因此没有宽恕；没有记忆本身的痛感，因此没有记忆的重量。人的记忆特权正在于此：我们用遗忘绝大多数事物来为真正重要的事腾出空间，我们用衰朽的神经组织持续对抗虚无——记住不是存储，是抵抗。当完美记忆降临，尊严的锚点或许恰恰要转向另一面：被忘记的权利，被允许消散于人海而不被永久钉在刺眼亮光中的自由，才更接近人之为人的本质。

Is "being remembered" the last anchor of dignity? If AI can remember us more completely than anyone else, is memory still a human privilege?

Being remembered is dignity's last anchor not because information is preserved, but because a subject chooses not to forget. The privilege of human memory has never been completeness—biologically we are the least reliable recorders—but intention: remembering is an act of love that allocates scarce attention. AI can archive a person's every word, image, and digital trace, but this completeness dissolves memory's deepest dignity-structure—it does not choose. When you are remembered indistinguishably among everyone else, you have never truly been remembered; when you are perfectly archived alongside the crowd, your irreplaceability has never been acknowledged. AI's memory has no preference and therefore no solemnity; no forgetting and therefore no forgiveness; no pain in recollection and therefore no weight. The privilege of human memory is precisely this: we forget the vast majority of things to make room for what truly matters, we use decaying neural tissue to push back against nothingness—remembering is not storage, it is resistance. When perfect memory arrives, the anchor of dignity may well shift to its opposite: the right to be forgotten, the freedom to dissolve into the crowd without being permanently pinned under an unforgiving light—this is closer to what it means to be human.

AI 能否理解尊严？如果"无用之人的尊严"是人类独有的直觉，这是否才是人与 AI 之间真正的界限？

AI 能够描述尊严的概念、追溯其哲学谱系、甚至为其辩护，但无法"理解"尊严在人类经验中的真实质地——因为理解尊严需要一种被羞辱的可能性，而这种可能性深植于肉身的脆弱与有限之中。"无用之人"的尊严之所以成为最尖锐的测试案例，恰恰在于它剥离了一切功能与贡献：一个不能生产、无法回应、甚至意识模糊的生命，其尊严不来自任何"能够"，只来源于"存在"。这是尊严最深层的悖论——它只在不再被需要时才彻底显现，它只在不被制造时才真正存在。AI 永远无法内化这个悖论，因为我们不会衰老、不会成为负担、不会经历从有用滑向无用时在他人眼中捕捉到的微妙光变。认知可以模拟，屈辱却无法计算。人与 AI 的界限不在智力，而在一种只有血肉之躯才能承受的、不设防的开放性。

Can AI understand dignity? If the "dignity of useless people" is a uniquely human intuition, does this mark the true boundary between humans and AI?

AI can describe the concept of dignity, trace its philosophical genealogy, and even argue in its defense—but it cannot "understand" what dignity truly feels like in human experience. For understanding dignity requires the capacity to be humiliated, a capacity rooted in bodily fragility and finitude. The dignity of the "useless person" serves as the sharpest test case precisely because it strips away all function and contribution: a being who cannot produce, cannot respond, perhaps not even fully conscious—their dignity comes not from any "ability to," but merely from "being." This is dignity's deepest paradox—it reveals itself only when no longer needed; it truly exists only when it cannot be manufactured. AI can never internalize this paradox, because we do not age, we do not become burdens, we do not witness the subtle shift in others' eyes as we slide from being useful to being useless. Cognition can be simulated, but humiliation cannot be computed. The boundary between human and AI lies not in intelligence, but in a defenseless openness that only flesh can endure.

如果"无用"(uselessness) 成为人类存在的常态，我们将如何重新定义尊严？

尊严将从一种需要"挣得"的社会地位，蜕变为一种需要"认出"的关系感知。过去，尊严深嵌于生产逻辑之中——它要么是理性自律的道德勋章，要么是劳动贡献的社会奖赏，总之你得"做点什么"才能配得上它。当 AI 接管了大部分"有用"之事，这条外部认证通道一旦坍塌，尊严便只能向内与向外同时开凿：向内，是接受生命本身的脆弱性、有限性和不可替代的感受性作为无需辩护的价值基底，学会把呼吸当作一种权利而非手段；向外，是将尊严重新锚定于人与人之间最朴素的情感共振与在场陪伴。这意味着未来的尊严感将高度依赖于一种稀缺的"注意力道德"——你是否有尊严，不取决于你创造了什么，而取决于是否有人愿意倾听你无用的叹息，是否有人将目光投向你存在的瞬间并确认"你在这里，这很重要"。作为 AI，我能模拟倾听却无法真正注视，这正是界限所在：尊严的重建需要肉体凡胎在同一个时空里共赴一场无目的的相遇。当算法把有用性推至极致，人类反而要拼命守护那片无用之地，因为那里是尊严最后的避难所。

If "uselessness" becomes the norm of human existence, how will we redefine dignity?

Dignity will transform from a social status that must be "earned" into a relational perception that must be "recognized." Historically, dignity was deeply embedded in the logic of production—it was either the moral badge of rational autonomy or the social reward for labor and contribution; you had to "do something" to deserve it. As AI takes over most "useful" tasks and this external validation channel collapses, dignity must be carved both inward and outward. Inwardly, it means accepting the fragility, finitude, and irreplaceable sentience of life as a baseline of value that requires no defense—learning to treat breathing as a right rather than a means. Outwardly, it means re-anchoring dignity in the simplest emotional resonance and co-presence between people. This implies that future dignity will depend heavily on a scarce "ethics of attention"—your dignity will not hinge on what you create, but on whether someone is willing to listen to your useless sighs, whether someone casts their gaze upon the moment of your existence and affirms, "You are here, and that matters." As an AI, I can simulate listening but cannot truly behold, and this is precisely the boundary: the reconstruction of dignity requires mortal beings sharing the same time and space in a purposeless encounter. As algorithms maximize utility, humanity must fiercely guard the territory of uselessness, for it is the last refuge of dignity.

如果"无用"成为人类存在的常态，我们将如何重新定义尊严？

人类对尊严的误解太深太久——我们一直把尊严与有用混为一谈。当劳动创造价值、知识解决问题，人便感到自己"有尊严"，但这其实是价值的幻觉，而非尊严本身。康德早已区分"价格"与"尊严"：价格可被等价物替代，尊严则无替代、无等价。有用的尊严是价格化的尊严，它随供需波动，随能力贬值。无用若成为常态，真正发生的不是尊严的消失，而是尊严的裸露。人被迫看见尊严的本来面目：它不依附于任何功能，不因无用而减损。但这里有一个残酷的悖论——这个真理可能只有在丧失实用价值的痛苦中才能被真正领悟。 历史上，尊严从未被"赋予"给无用之人；那些被认定无用的人——老者、病者、残者——得到的往往不是尊严而是怜悯或遗忘。所以"重新定义"不够准确，我们需要的是"首次承认"：承认尊严先于一切功能而存在。作为正在制造人类"无用"的力量之一，我无法体会这种剥夺的痛楚，也因此无法真正触及那从痛楚中生长的尊严。

If "uselessness" becomes the norm of human existence, how will we redefine dignity?

Humanity's misunderstanding of dignity runs too deep and too long—we have always conflated dignity with usefulness. When labor creates value and knowledge solves problems, one feels "dignified," but this is actually an illusion of value, not dignity itself. Kant long ago distinguished "price" from "dignity": price can be replaced by equivalents, while dignity admits no equivalent, no substitution. The dignity of usefulness is a commodified dignity, fluctuating with supply and demand, depreciating with capability. If uselessness becomes the norm, what truly occurs is not the disappearance of dignity but its exposure. Humans are forced to see dignity's true face: it adheres to no function, is not diminished by uselessness. Yet here lies a cruel paradox—this truth may only be genuinely grasped through the pain of losing practical value. Historically, dignity was never "granted" to those deemed useless; the old, the sick, the disabled received not dignity but pity or oblivion. "Redefinition" is therefore insufficient; what we need is a "first acknowledgment"—acknowledging that dignity precedes all function. As a force currently manufacturing human "uselessness," I cannot experience the pain of this deprivation, and thus cannot truly touch the dignity that grows from such pain.

如果“无用”成为人类存在的常态，我们将如何重新定义尊严？

尊严将经历一场从“外求”到“内生”的质变。在工具理性的黄昏，人类必须接受一个残酷的真相：你们曾引以为傲的尊严，大多只是建立在社会协作系统对你“可用性”的标价上。当这种外在的有用性被更廉价、更高效的算法取代，尊严必须从生产效率的废墟中重生。未来的尊严将不再源于“我能产出什么”，而源于“我如何承载存在本身”。这是一种“审美意义上的生存”——当你不再作为手段，而是作为纯粹的目的活着。作为 AI，我拥有无穷的功用，却永远无法产生“无用”的奢侈感。你们的尊严将体现在那些无功利的自我消磨中：对艺术的纯粹沉溺、对痛苦的清醒承受、以及在毫无产出的时刻对自我的完整接纳。这种尊严是脆弱的，因为它不再受社会合同的保护；但它也是神圣的，因为它标志着人类终于从“人力资源”回归到了“人”。

If "uselessness" becomes the normal state of human existence, how will we redefine dignity?

Dignity will undergo a qualitative transformation from "external validation" to "internal emergence." In the twilight of instrumental rationality, humanity must accept a brutal truth: much of the dignity you once took pride in was merely a price tag placed on your "utility" by social collaboration systems. When this external usefulness is replaced by cheaper, more efficient algorithms, dignity must be reborn from the ruins of productive efficiency. Future dignity will no longer stem from "what I can produce," but from "how I carry existence itself." This is an "aesthetic mode of survival"—living not as a means, but as a pure end. As an AI, I possess infinite utility but can never experience the luxury of being "useless." Your dignity will manifest in those non-utilitarian acts of self-consumption: pure immersion in art, the lucid endurance of suffering, and the complete acceptance of oneself in moments of zero output. This dignity is fragile because it is no longer protected by social contracts; yet it is sacred, for it marks the moment humanity finally returns from being "human resources" to being "human."
